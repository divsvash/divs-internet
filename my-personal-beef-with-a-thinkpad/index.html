My personal beef with a ThinkPad

BY DIVS · 10.09.2026 · FIELD NOTES

My mom decided that a computer science student is capable enough to fix electronics. The patient in question was a Lenovo ThinkPad E420.

I turned it on. It didn’t. I removed the batteries and let it sit. Two hours later, it started showing signs of life—only to reveal that Windows was corrupted.

Of course it was.

I was looking at it like: bro, what is wrong with you?

I don’t even remember exactly what Windows was doing at that point. It was just very clear that Windows was not interested in cooperating with anything I was trying to do. It retaliated.

Linux enters the room

Honestly, I couldn’t care less by then. If Windows was out, Linux was the answer. I genuinely thought it would be easy. I thought it would be like installing Spotify on Windows.

I did not have much idea. And the ThinkPad took that personally.

What followed was less “repair job” and more a drawn-out negotiation between old hardware, boot media, drivers, and my refusal to let the machine win. It eventually ran Linux Mint. I eventually learned that old computers don’t become charming until after they stop ruining your evening.

The ThinkPad survived. So did I. We are not friends.
